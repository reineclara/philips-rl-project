\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work addresses the optimization of contractual coverage for medical
equipment, with the operational goals of reducing the fraction of uncovered
equipment and improving contractual decision-making. The problem is
formalized as a Markov decision process and solved with the PPO algorithm,
trained on a simulation environment built in the absence of direct access to
Philips France's operational data.
On $20$ evaluation episodes, the PPO agent reaches an average reward of
$55.08$, substantially above the random policy ($-184.91$) and close to the
heuristic reference ($60.56$), attesting that a relevant coverage policy is
learned from simulated interactions alone, without any explicit business
rule.
The residual advantage of the heuristic reflects the structural simplicity
of the considered environment, in which a one-dimensional rule on the time
to expiration is near-optimal; the added value of reinforcement learning is
expected to grow with the complexity and variability of real environments,
where no simple rule can jointly capture dependencies between cost, risk and
temporal dynamics.
These results nonetheless rely on a deliberately simplified simulator, a
four-dimensional state representation and a training budget of $3{,}000$
episodes, all of which bound the scope of the conclusions.
Natural extensions include the exploitation of real operational data to
enrich the state representation, the extension of the framework to
heterogeneous portfolios subject to global budget constraints, a
substantially longer training horizon, and ultimately a supervised
deployment in production.
The presented approach demonstrates the viability of driving contractual
decisions through reinforcement learning and opens the way to a gradual
adoption of learned policies, complementary to existing business rules.
