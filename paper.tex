\begin{abstract}
Hospital equipment management at medical-technology companies such as
Philips relies on a complex portfolio of maintenance contracts,
manufacturer warranties and warranty extensions. Current decision-
support tools remain largely descriptive and provide limited
anticipation of contractual risks. We cast the coverage-optimization
problem as a finite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP) on a single
piece of equipment and train a from-scratch Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO) agent in a reproducible simulation environment. Our contribution
is not algorithmic: PPO, GAE and observation normalization are taken
off-the-shelf; we contribute a clean MDP formulation for a
decision-making problem that, to our knowledge, has not been cast as RL
in the published literature, together with a statistically rigorous evaluation
protocol that now includes synchronous value iteration (\texttt{dp\_solver.py})
as a tabular Bellman reference on an augmented finite MDP. On $100$ shared seeds,
PPO attains $53.14 \pm 4.68$ ($95\%$ CI $[52.22,\,54.06]$), far above random
($-241.44 \pm 92.66$), below a threshold heuristic ($58.06 \pm 3.93$; Welch
$t \approx{-}8.06$, $p < 10^{-4}$), and below synchronous VI rollout
statistics (${\approx}58.08 \pm 4.0$, mean nearly identical because VI and heuristic
Welch-tests $p \approx 0.97$). Interpretationally, RL competes against co-optimal
planning benchmarks, not an underspecified folklore rule.

The heuristic remains robust across its threshold sweep ($49.14$--$58.06$); PPO dispersion on pooled seeds matches the heuristic order of magnitude while the mean shortfall persists. Extensions span richer states, heterogeneous portfolios,
offline histories, tighter PPO hyper-parameter ablations---avenues where greedy tabulation loses tractability faster than amortized RL.
\end{abstract}
