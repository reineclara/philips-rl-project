\begin{abstract}
Hospital equipment management at medical-technology companies such as
Philips relies on a complex portfolio of maintenance contracts,
manufacturer warranties and warranty extensions. Current decision-
support tools remain largely descriptive and provide limited
anticipation of contractual risks. We cast the coverage-optimization
problem as a finite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP) on a single
piece of equipment and train a from-scratch Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO) agent in a reproducible simulation environment. Our contribution
is not algorithmic: PPO, GAE and observation normalization are taken
off-the-shelf; we contribute a clean MDP formulation for a
decision-making problem that, to our knowledge, has not been cast as RL
in the published literature, together with a statistically rigorous
evaluation protocol. On $100$ evaluation episodes drawn from a shared
seed pool, PPO reaches a mean reward of $55.08 \pm 3.62$ (95\% CI
$[54.4,\,55.8]$), significantly above a random baseline ($-184.91 \pm
12.80$) and significantly below a threshold-based heuristic ($60.56 \pm
3.10$; Welch's $t$-test $p \ll 0.01$). A sensitivity sweep over the
heuristic's renewal threshold shows that the reported heuristic sits
almost exactly at the peak of its one-parameter family. We analyze this
gap, discuss the limitations of the current setup, and outline the
extensions---richer state representations, heterogeneous portfolios,
offline training on operational logs, non-stationary dynamics---under
which reinforcement learning is expected to provide a clearer advantage
over hand-designed rules.
\end{abstract}
