\begin{abstract}
Hospital equipment management at medical-technology companies such as
Philips relies on a complex portfolio of maintenance contracts,
manufacturer warranties and warranty extensions. Current decision-
support tools remain largely descriptive and provide limited
anticipation of contractual risks. We cast the coverage-optimization
problem as a finite-horizon Markov decision process (MDP) on a single
piece of equipment and train a from-scratch Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO) agent in a reproducible simulation environment. Our contribution
is not algorithmic: PPO, GAE and observation normalization are taken
off-the-shelf; we contribute a clean MDP formulation for a
decision-making problem that, to our knowledge, has not been cast as RL
in the published literature, together with a statistically rigorous
evaluation protocol. On $100$ evaluation episodes drawn from a shared
seed pool, PPO reaches a mean reward of $44.83 \pm 20.05$ (95\% CI
$[40.90,\,48.76]$), significantly above a random baseline
($-241.44 \pm 92.66$) and significantly below a threshold-based
heuristic ($58.06 \pm 3.93$; Welch's two-sided $t$-test $t = -6.48$,
$p < 10^{-4}$). PPO's standard deviation is more than five times that
of the heuristic, indicating residual training instability. A
sensitivity sweep over the heuristic's renewal threshold shows that
the rule is robust across its operating range ($49.14$ to $58.06$
over thresholds $3$--$15$), and that PPO is currently below all five
tested thresholds. We interpret this as a quantitative statement about
the regime in which a simple threshold rule already captures most of
the available return, and outline the extensions---richer state
representations, heterogeneous portfolios, offline training on
operational logs, non-stationary dynamics---under which reinforcement
learning is expected to provide a clearer advantage over hand-designed
rules.
\end{abstract}
